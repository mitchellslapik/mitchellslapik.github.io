<p>
I am an MD/PhD candidate at <a href="https://gsbs.uth.edu/mdphd/">McGovern Medical School</a> and a systems and computational neuroscientist studying how patterns of neural activity shape behavior. I want to bring these approaches to psychiatric illness.
</p>

<p>
In Valentin Dragoi's laboratory, I used large-scale electrophysiology in nonhuman primates to investigate how behavioral state changes neural representations and communication between visual and prefrontal cortex. My co-first-author work showing that locomotion enhances this communication, improving prefrontal coding and visual detection, has been accepted in principle at <em>Nature Neuroscience</em>. I also study how prefrontal neurons represent the body and have identified reproducible structure in human 7-T fMRI responses that leading vision models fail to capture. My research has appeared in <em>Neural Computation</em> and the <em>Journal of Vision</em>, with support from <a href="https://researchtraining.nih.gov/programs/fellowships/F30">NIH F30</a> and <a href="https://www.uth.edu/ccts/education-training/tl1-program">TL1</a> fellowships.
</p>

<p>
Two patients can have the same symptoms and be headed in opposite directions. A clinical score tells us where someone is, but not how they got there. My current research examines recovery and subsequent vulnerability across longitudinal cohorts. I want to understand what changes before relapse, what distinguishes recovery, and why the same treatment helps one patient but not another.
</p>

<p>
Before medicine, I studied philosophy in the Honors Program at <a href="https://www.swarthmore.edu/">Swarthmore College</a> and the neuropsychiatric consequences of cerebellar disease at <a href="https://www.jhu.edu/">Johns Hopkins University</a>.
</p>
