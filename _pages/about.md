<p>
I am an MD/PhD candidate at <a href="https://gsbs.uth.edu/mdphd/">McGovern Medical School</a> and a systems and computational neuroscientist interested in how brain activity gives rise to behavior, how those relationships change with context, and how these approaches can help us understand psychiatric illness.
</p>

<p>
During my PhD in Valentin Dragoi's laboratory, I used large-scale electrophysiology in nonhuman primates to study population coding and communication between visual and prefrontal cortex during natural behavior. One focus of this work has been how behavioral state changes neural representation and interareal communication. I have also studied how biological and artificial neural systems transform sensory information, including work on representational untangling and the relationship between retinal and cortical complexity. More recently, I have extended these questions to human 7-T fMRI and modern vision models. My work has been published in <em>Neural Computation</em> and <em>Journal of Vision</em>, with a co-first-author manuscript accepted in principle at <em>Nature Neuroscience</em>.
</p>

<p>
My interest in brain-behavior relationships began in human neuroscience. Before my doctoral training, I spent three years at <a href="https://www.jhu.edu/">Johns Hopkins University</a> studying cognitive and neuropsychiatric manifestations of cerebellar ataxia, including visuospatial function, emotional symptoms, and quality of life. That work resulted in several publications in <em>The Cerebellum</em>, including <em>Neuropsychiatric Symptoms as a Reliable Phenomenology of Cerebellar Ataxia</em>.
</p>

<p>
I now hope to bring the tools of systems and computational neuroscience to psychiatry. I am particularly interested in why patients with the same diagnosis can follow different courses and respond differently to treatment. Rather than treating psychiatric illness as a static label, I want to understand how symptoms, behavior, and neural activity evolve over time, what mechanisms drive transitions toward relapse or recovery, and whether those dynamics can ultimately improve prognosis and treatment selection.
</p>

<p>
My research training has been supported first by a <a href="https://www.uth.edu/ccts/education-training/tl1-program">TL1 Translational and Clinical Science Predoctoral Fellowship</a> and subsequently by an <a href="https://researchtraining.nih.gov/programs/fellowships/F30">NIH F30 Fellowship</a>. Before medicine, I studied philosophy in the Honors Program at <a href="https://www.swarthmore.edu/">Swarthmore College</a>, where I became interested in questions about mind, agency, and subjective experience that continue to shape my interest in psychiatry. Alongside my research and medical training, I have worked as a crisis counselor.
</p>

<p>
I am currently applying to research-focused psychiatry residency programs, with the long-term goal of becoming an academic psychiatrist and independent physician-scientist in computational psychiatry.
</p>
